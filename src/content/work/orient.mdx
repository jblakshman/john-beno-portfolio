## Brief

As part of a fictional agency pitch, I was challenged to redesign the visual identity of an existing brand undergoing a strategic repositioning. I chose the Japanese watchmaker Orient. The brand is known in enthusiast circles for offering great value at an affordable price, but it lacks a strong design presence and an emotional identity.

The goal was to transform Orient into a modern, high-horology brand while keeping an authentic sense of craftsmanship. The deliverables included a full redesign of the logo, color palette and typography, a website and other digital applications such as marketing material and a stripped-down brand guideline.

## Idea

Rather than pushing Orient into the luxury space by simply making it look more expensive, I focused on building a visual and emotional language that captures the feeling of being inside a boutique, where time slows down, textures matter and craftsmanship is tangible.

My concept was to balance elegance and modernity. Many luxury brands lean heavily into tradition and conservatism, which can feel outdated. My aim was a fresh, fluid and elevated identity that feels both sophisticated and current.

## Process

To achieve this, I reimagined the entire visual system of the brand.

### Logo redesign

I designed a refined logomark that can stand confidently on a watch dial, paired with a strong, modern wordmark. The logo animation, which I used as a splash screen in the mobile application, reflects the precision and calm associated with high horology.

<Figure src={logomark} alt="Brand guideline page showing the construction of the new Orient logomark, a calligraphic O and R." />

### Color and typography

The palette is built on neutral tones like soft ivory and charcoal gray, with muted secondary tones that carry a sense of depth and calm across all media. The typography uses Monday Sans, a sans-serif that subtly echoes the elegance of serif fonts. It is paired with Tangerine, a handwritten accent font used very sparingly, which adds a handcrafted feel without overwhelming the clarity of the design.

<Figure src={palette} alt="Color palette page with ivory, mauve, charcoal, slate blue and deep green swatches." />

### Visual language

Images don’t just sit next to text. They live within textured layers like velvet, which I placed behind each picture to evoke the sensory experience of holding a timepiece in a boutique. This layering adds visual richness while keeping a sense of lightness and space.

### Website

The interface is purposefully calm and unhurried, with no aggressive calls to action and no immediate shopping prompts. The homepage has no button in the hero section, to avoid the digital equivalent of a pushy sales associate. Instead, a contact page lets visitors request a boutique visit, and a boutique page invites them to explore the world of Orient in person. This mirrors how luxury watches are usually experienced: physically, intentionally and with care.

### Prototype and assets

All visual work was done in Figma. I used Spline to build a 3D version of the logomark with custom lighting, then composited it onto high-end watch renders to simulate how the logomark might look on an actual product. I used Affinity Photo to edit the mockups shown here.

<Figure src={logomarkOnWatch} alt="Close-up render of a diamond-set chronograph dial carrying the new Orient logomark." />

### Brand guidelines

To tie everything together, I created a streamlined brand guidelines PDF documenting logo usage, color and type systems, image style and applications across social media.

## Reflection

This project was a solo effort from ideation to final prototype. It challenged me to think not just visually but also experimentally. Making someone feel like they are holding something precious through a screen was an engaging and reflective challenge.

I learned that simplicity is very powerful, and that thoughtful layering and visual rhythm can elevate even the most static screens. Every small element, like a font pairing or a button state, contributes to how a brand is perceived emotionally.
