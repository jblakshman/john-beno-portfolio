## Brief

The goal of this project was to explore the theme “Active / Passive” in any format or medium. There were no technical or creative constraints, only the open invitation to create something that reflects this duality in a meaningful way.

Instead of interpreting the theme through interface design, I approached it conceptually by combining my interests in music, storytelling and sensory perception. The project became an opportunity to experiment freely and build something I personally cared about.

## Idea

While brainstorming around active versus passive, I became fascinated by how the same stimulus can be experienced in completely different ways depending on how it is framed.

I kept coming back to perception, and how our brains often process one thing actively while something just as important is received passively. One example was the brand Air Up, which tricks the brain into “tasting” flavored water by engaging the sense of smell while drinking. This inspired me to test a hypothesis: music plays a huge emotional role when we watch a video clip, even if we don’t actively focus on it. So I composed four distinct soundtracks for the same clip to show how deeply audio changes our emotional response to visuals.

## Process

To bring the idea to life, I looked for a short, copyright-free clip with neutral visuals and edited it down to about 40 seconds: short enough to watch four times without fatigue, long enough to build emotional momentum.

Then I composed and produced four different tracks for the exact same visuals, each designed to provoke a distinct emotional response. All the music was composed and arranged in Ableton Live, and I edited the four versions in DaVinci Resolve.

### Experience design

Since this was an experiment, I wanted a space where people could experience it firsthand, not just read about it. So I designed and published a small website for the project.

I started by drawing a soundwave-like background in Illustrator. Then I built a simple scrolling website in Wix Studio that embeds the four video versions, each with a short intro explaining the idea behind it. Visitors watch the same visuals in completely different emotional contexts and can reflect on how the music changed their reaction.

## Reflection

This project showed me how multimodal design, here combining audio, video and interaction, can shape powerful emotional experiences. It reminded me that user experience goes beyond interfaces. It is about emotion, perception and engagement, whatever the format.
