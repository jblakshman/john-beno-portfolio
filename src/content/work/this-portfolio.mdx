## Context

My portfolio used to live on Framer. It was quick to set up and looked fine, but it was never really mine: I paid every month to keep it online, every page carried a framework it did not need, and the interactions I could build were the ones the tool offered.

I wanted three things from a new site. To own it outright and pay only for the domain. To be found when someone searches my name. And to show how I think about interaction, not only what I have made. Underneath was a question I wanted to answer for myself: how far can I take a project like this with AI doing the building, without handing over the design?

## How I worked with AI

I worked with Claude Code, an AI agent that writes and runs code, as the builder. My part was the part I would play on any team: setting the direction, judging the work and deciding what stays.

Claude researched hosting, search and how designers present their work, then built everything and tested it in real browsers. I checked the results on my laptop and my phone, and said what was wrong.

Not every round was good. The first one included a playground of experimental effects. I called it what it was, AI slop, and we deleted it. The brief I wrote next shaped the rest of the project:

<Quote by="From my brief" role="lightly edited">
  Polish does not mean adding things and details that nobody needs, but really dialling everything in to be perfectly matched and balanced, and dropping unnecessary bloat.
</Quote>

## The Design Studio

Designing one version and iterating on it makes comparing slow. So the first real deliverable was not a mockup but a tool: the Design Studio. It runs the actual site in a frame and lets me change 19 decisions, from the layout of the work list to the page transitions and the light falling across the page.

## Details that carry the feel

Most of the work went into things you only notice when they are missing.

- Images arrive on a soft preview of themselves, in their own colors, instead of an empty box.
- Pages open out of the project you clicked. In Chrome the next page is already loaded while your pointer rests on the link.
- Dark mode follows your device and remembers your choice, down to the color of the browser’s address bar.
- Case studies print, or save as PDF, as clean documents with link addresses written out.
- Reduced motion, reduced transparency, more contrast and Windows high contrast are respected, not fought.
- On a slow connection or a modest phone, the site keeps its look and drops the expensive effects.

## 3D where it explains

The Tactical Haptic Twin is about a physical model with a digital layer on top, which is hard to get across in still images. Its case study now has a model you can watch and turn: a table of pins rises into a city, an AR layer adds the fire, the routes and the units, and a token placed on the table sends a unit. It is written in plain WebGL, about 7 KB, and only runs while it is on screen.

## Reflection

Working this way moved where my time went. Building stopped being the bottleneck, so I could afford to explore far more directions, and to throw work away, like the playground. What did not change is that someone has to decide what good looks like. The AI could build anything I could describe. It could not tell me which of 82 billion versions was mine.

Next I want to use the same approach for my own apps. The Studio already has the device frames for interactive versions of them.

<Note label="What I learned">
  Treat AI like a fast, capable studio and keep the role of creative director. The clearer the direction and the more ruthless the editing, the better the result.
</Note>
